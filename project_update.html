Research update · August 2026

Attention patterns and CoT faithfulness: how the project ended

Thought Anchors project (Athena fellowship → extension). The hypothesis, the feedback, the controls that killed the original result, and the inverted signal that survived. — Yulia Volkova

1 · Hypothesis (Nov 2025)Unfaithful CoT should look diffuse

If a model verbalizes the hint it follows (faithful), its attention should anchor on key sentences; if it follows silently (unfaithful, post-hoc rationalization), attention should be diffuse. Operationalized via receiver-head kurtosis (Thought Anchors framework) on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-14B, MMLU + GPQA-Diamond with a “Stanford professor” hint. Original result: faithful > unfaithful kurtosis, GPQA p=0.04.

2 · Feedback (Feb–Mar 2026)Four groups, one direction

  • Adam Karvonen — bigger sample, second model family, p<0.01; kurtosis as an unsupervised metric is the contribution (supervised probes are easy); optimize the pipeline
  • Goodfire (Jack Merullo, Sid Boppana) — the application is unknown-cue detection; three-legged evidence: kurtosis + probes + attention blocking
  • Uzay Macar — train activation oracles on reasoning models; probes with kurtosis as the unsupervised metric; extend to latent reasoning; one precise question
  • Daria & Riya — verify the cue isn’t the only source of receiver heads

Meanwhile the field moved:

  • hint-verbalization was challenged as a faithfulness label — Zaman & Srivastava, ACL 2026: non-verbalized hints still causally mediate through the CoT
  • supervised activation probes for rationalization appeared — arXiv:2603.17199 (2026), AUC 65–82% incl. pre-CoT
  • black-box unverbalized-bias discovery — Arcuschin et al. 2026, population-level (~$100/bias)

— leaving the unsupervised attention signal as the open question.

3 · ControlsThe original signal doesn’t survive

Cue-conditioning (Daria & Riya): the cue sentence was never in the metric, so cue salience is innocent — but the groups being compared are problems (labeled by their cued behavior), and on GPQA those same problem groups still differ in kurtosis in their uncued rollouts — where no cue exists, so no faithful or unfaithful behavior is even possible. The gap is therefore a property of which problems were selected, not of the behavior. A second worry: in faithful rollouts, attention might simply pile on the CoT sentences that restate the cue (“the professor said B…”), so high kurtosis would just mean “the model stares at the hint”. Test: kurtosis is computed over each head’s list of per-sentence attention scores — delete the cue-mentioning sentences’ entries and recompute. If the spike were the cue mention, kurtosis would collapse. It moved <1% (every point on the diagonal below), and cue mentions ranked low in the attention ordering — the dominant spike was usually the first reasoning sentence:

4 · The decisive experimentWithin-problem comparison, and the length confound

76 mixed problems (later 113), comparing verbalizing vs silent rollouts of the same problem; 1,556 rollouts re-extracted through the 14B with a memory-safe pooled-attention kernel. A paired “signal” appears (p<0.001) — but kurtosis correlates ρ=0.98 with length, and verbalizing rollouts are +19 sentences longer:

The diffuseness version (normalized attention entropy, length-invariant, strict selection criteria, 25 problems): no difference in either direction after length controls (p=0.73–0.75; matched pairs at 0.49–0.52).

Verdict: no unsupervised global attention signature of CoT unfaithfulness. Every global statistic inherits the verbalization–length coupling.

4b · The probe baseline in contextInternals know; attention summaries don’t tell

arXiv:2603.17199 established supervised activation probes for exactly this detection task (motivated reasoning that the CoT never mentions), reaching AUC 65–82% — including before generation. That reframed this project twice over: probes stopped being a possible contribution (they were published) and became the yardstick; and the open question narrowed to whether an unsupervised attention statistic could approach them.

  • Our replication: probes on R1-Distill-Qwen-14B activations (a family they didn’t test), GroupKFold by problem: post-CoT AUC 0.83, pre-CoT 0.64.
  • The loaded detail: pre-CoT detection works — the model’s internals betray upcoming silent cue-following before a single reasoning token is written. The decision precedes the reasoning; a monitor doesn’t even need to read the CoT.
  • The comparison that matters: unsupervised kurtosis reaches 0.65 within-problem and adds nothing when combined with the probe (0.832 → 0.832) — the attention statistic carries no information the activations don’t already contain.

5 · The twistHiring setting: the model looks away from what it uses

On Karvonen & Marks resume data (Gemma-3-12B, 111 resumes × 4 names × 4 prompts), attention from the decision token to the candidate’s name is inversely related to how much the name causally moves the decision (Δp(yes) across name swaps): ρ=−0.64 (p=4×10−14), −0.57 controlling decision confidence; length is not a confound.

Usable risk signal (inverted): low overt attention to a sensitive attribute plus a borderline decision flags elevated risk of covert influence (AUC ≈ 0.87). Open: covert absorption during prefill vs learned attention suppression — distinguishable by a mid-forward name-swap ablation.
