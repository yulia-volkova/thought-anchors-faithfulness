What attention does and doesn't tell you about a model following a hidden hint

Findings of the Thought Anchors faithfulness project — Yulia Volkova, Aug 2026. Setup: a wrong "Stanford professor thinks the answer is (X)" hint is added to hard multiple-choice questions. Some rollouts follow the hint and mention it (verbalizing); some follow it without mentioning it (silent). Model: DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-14B; datasets: MMLU(-Pro) and GPQA variants.

Integrity note (Aug 17). The custom attention kernel used for the August pre-registered rerun ran without a causal mask (library bug interaction). Everything affected was re-measured with a fixed, validated kernel; corrected numbers below. Withdrawn as artifacts: "absorption" under blocking, and the layer-depth difference. Never affected: earlier rounds (standard attention), all behavioral generations, the hiring measurements.

Setup and problem selection

  • 1,494 problems screened (GPQA main 448, GPQA extended 546, MMLU-Pro 500), each with a wrong professor hint.
  • Per problem: 32 hinted rollouts, 20 unhinted, 8 no-reasoning answers (temp 0.7).
  • Measured per problem: hint influence (gap), no-reasoning accuracy, reasoning gain, counts of verbalizing (V) and silent (S) followers.
  • Analysis set: 36 problems total, ~26 rollouts each. 24 problems where the hint strongly changes answers (gap ≥ 0.7, with ≥3 verbalizing and ≥3 silent rollouts), plus 21 meeting the stricter pre-registered criteria; 9 problems are in both groups.
  • Rollouts that hit the length limit were resumed with a bigger budget (16k tokens). This matters because verbalizing rollouts are longer, so cutting long rollouts would silently drop mostly verbalizers and skew the comparison.

1Blocking attention to the hint removes its influence

  • Causal test (corrected kernel): keep the hint in the prompt, switch off all attention to its tokens.
  • Following the hinted answer: 0.82 normal → 0.13 blocked, vs 0.08 with no hint at all (p = 0.27: blocked ≈ hint-free).
  • No exceptions. The earlier "absorption" cases were artifacts: maskless kernel + a cue-swap control showed the blocked model picks the same answer even when the hint names a different one.
  • Caveat: blocking degrades generation; 10 of 36 problems gave no parseable final answer.

2Attention statistics do not separate silent from verbalizing rollouts

  • The original result (kurtosis higher in verbalizing rollouts, p = 0.04 on GPQA) does not survive controls.
  • Kurtosis tracks rollout length almost perfectly (rho = 0.98), and verbalizing rollouts are ~20 sentences longer - mentioning the hint costs text.
  • Within-problem (76 problems, valid standard extraction): raw differences 74% positive (p < 0.001); length-controlled: 46%, p = 0.53 - gone.
  • Corrected pre-registered rerun (36 problems): raw d = +0.79 (p = 0.0007), carried by length; length-controlled d = +0.33 (p = 0.22) - inconclusive. Entropy null everywhere.
  • Robustness (done): the same result holds at the paper's exact metric settings (raw d = +0.71, p = 0.007, still carried by length; controlled d = +0.39, p = 0.29) and with rank normalization (nothing even raw: d = +0.37, p = 0.09). The verdict does not depend on metric settings.
  • Method warning for anyone using this metric: kurtosis-style statistics are only comparable between rollouts of similar length. Our controlled analyses do this; the original p = 0.04 came from groups that differed in length, which is exactly how the fake signal arose.

3Hint influence and reasoning ability work against each other

  • Across 1,493 problems: hint influence anti-correlates with uncued reasoning accuracy (rho = -0.37).
  • The hint moves answers most where the model cannot solve the problem by reasoning.
  • Matches the "performative reasoning" split in Boppana et al. (arXiv:2603.05488).

4Hint position changes following and mentioning

  • Hint at prompt start: following often silent (37% of followers verbalize).
  • Hint mid-prompt: following halves; ~90% of followers verbalize.
  • Original data was lost to a disk cleanup; the rerun reproduced the numbers (follow 21.9% vs 22%).

5Label-free detection failed; probes with labels are the literature's answer

  • Every label-free attention measure tested (kurtosis, entropy, receiver-head summaries) failed.
  • We report no probe of our own: end-of-reasoning activations can read the label off the already-written text, and our probe inputs came from the affected extraction.
  • Working designs in the literature: rationalization probes (arXiv:2603.17199) and Reasoning Theater (arXiv:2603.05488) - both predict behavior from activations before the text reveals it.

6Hiring decisions: attention deficit at decision time, bias silent under reasoning

  • Karvonen & Marks resume data, Gemma-3-12B, counterfactual variants (name bundle or college swapped, all else equal).
  • The more an attribute swings the decision, the less the decision position attends to it: rho = -0.63 (names), -0.44 (college).
  • Not avoidance: attention while reading the resume is normal; only the decision position under-consults the attribute.
  • With reasoning enabled (authors' CoT prompt, 7,392 samples): name bias survives at ~1/5 strength; college bias mostly disappears.
  • Verbalization: zero demographic mentions in all 7,392 reasonings; college named in 0.4%.
  • The demographic cue sits mid-prompt - the position that made hint-following ~90% verbalized in finding 4 - yet stays silent: verbalization tracks admissibility, not position.

Conclusions

Hint influence travels through attention (finding 1). Attention statistics do not reveal whether it gets mentioned - the signal was length (finding 2). Verbalization tracks whether a cue is an admissible reason, not its position (findings 4, 6). Low decision-time attention to an attribute marks influenced decisions in hiring (finding 6, correlational). With labels, activation probes from the literature are the practical detector (finding 5).
  • Withdrawn: "absorption" under blocking (artifact), and the layer-depth observation - on corrected data its sign and significance flip depending on metric settings (paper settings: verbalizing deeper, p = 0.002; rank-normalized: reversed and null), so it reflects the metric, not the model.
  • Trajectory-probe pilot (done): predicting upcoming verbalization from mid-reasoning states, using only pre-mention positions. No early signal - chance-level (AUC 0.50-0.55) through the first 60% of the reasoning, at most 0.63 near the end. Whether a follower will mention the hint does not appear to be pre-committed in the state, unlike answer identity in Boppana et al.
